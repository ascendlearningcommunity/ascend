Why Critical Thinking?

Think Clearly. Decide Wisely.

Critical thinking helps you understand information, solve problems, avoid assumptions and make logical decisions.

Analyze Information

Break information into smaller parts and understand what the facts actually show.

Evaluate Evidence

Compare evidence, identify reliable information and separate facts from opinions.

Solve Problems

Explore possible solutions, compare alternatives and choose a reasoned approach.

Core Skills

Six Habits of a Critical Thinker

Build the mental habits required to think independently and make evidence-based decisions.

Observation

Notice important details, patterns and information before forming conclusions.

Questioning

Ask useful questions such as what, why, how and what evidence supports the claim.

Analysis

Break complex information into smaller parts and understand how the parts connect.

Evaluation

Check evidence, sources, assumptions and reasoning before accepting a conclusion.

Problem Solving

Generate possible solutions and compare their advantages, disadvantages and consequences.

Decision Making

Use evidence and logical reasoning to select a well-considered course of action.

Practice

Critical Thinking Activities

Practice thinking through real situations instead of only learning definitions.

Fact or Opinion?

Take statements from everyday sources and identify whether they are facts, opinions or claims requiring evidence.

Question Challenge

Choose a topic and create useful questions beginning with What, Why and How.

Pros & Cons

Choose a decision and create a balanced list of advantages, disadvantages and possible consequences.

Source Investigation

Compare multiple sources and identify what evidence supports their claims.

Problem Challenge

Choose an everyday problem and develop multiple possible solutions before selecting one.

Structured Debate

Study different viewpoints and support your position with clear reasoning and evidence.

Study Tips

Build the Habit of Thinking

Critical thinking improves through regular practice.

Ask “Why?”

Do not immediately accept information. Ask why the claim should be believed and what evidence supports it.

Separate Fact From Opinion

Practice identifying which statements can be verified and which represent personal viewpoints.

Consider Multiple Viewpoints

Try to understand different perspectives before forming a conclusion.

Use Evidence

Support important conclusions with reliable information rather than assumptions.

Think Before Reacting

Give yourself time to understand a situation before making a decision.

Reflect on Decisions

After making a decision, review what worked, what did not and what you could improve.

Think Better. Solve Better.

Build critical thinking through observation, questioning, analysis, evidence and continuous practice.
